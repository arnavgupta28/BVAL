# BVAL country market factors: developed markets vs US (UK, CA, AU, DE, FR, plus ES/IT/UAE briefly)

Research date: 2026-11-22. BVAL's US baseline: CPM per 1K views of Reels $20, TikTok $12, Shorts $8, YouTube integration $22. Tiers: nano 1-10K, micro 10-100K, mid 100-500K, macro 500K-1M, mega 1M+.

Caveat that applies to everything below: **no source publishes a country x tier x platform price matrix built from transaction data.** The best transactional source (Kolect, 2026) gives country medians and price per 1K views, but not by tier. The local rate cards are mostly blog or agency estimates. Many are USD figures converted to local currency, and their tier boundaries differ from BVAL's. The tier-level factors in the last section are therefore **derived** and partly judgment-based.

---

## 1. Typical per-post prices in local currency by tier and platform (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Local rate cards exist for every target market but disagree by 2-10x within a single country. Several "local" cards are just USD benchmarks run through an FX conversion. They are useful for sanity checks, not for calibration. The one transaction-based local dataset (Matriochka, France) shows micro-creator prices tripling between 2025 and H1 2026.

### Cited Findings

**United Kingdom (GBP)**
- Kolsquare (2025 budget piece) gives Instagram per-post rates of nano (1-10K) £50-£200, micro (10-100K) £200-£1,000, macro (100K-1M) £1,000-£10,000, and mega (1M+) £10,000+. TikTok nano is £100-£300, and the piece says TikTok "generally higher" than Instagram. Mega TikTok is £15,000+ per campaign. These figures come from search-result summaries of Kolsquare pages. — [Kolsquare forecasts & budgets](https://www.kolsquare.com/en/free-ressources/influencer-marketing-forecasts-and-budgets); [Kolsquare 2025 cost](https://www.kolsquare.com/en/blog/how-much-does-influencer-marketing-cost-in-2025)
- A different Kolsquare page uses other tier boundaries: nano (1-5K) £10-£100 and micro (5-100K) £100-£1,000. — [Kolsquare search tool page](https://www.kolsquare.com/en/influencer-search-tool)
- Kolsquare's 2027 budget report gives the median *estimated media value* (EMV, not a fee) for UK micro creators: £513 per TikTok video and £2,552 per YouTube video. — [Kolsquare budget report 2027](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/free-resources/budget-report-2027-influencer-marketing)
- Awin (20 Jan 2026) has a UK section with no platform split and no stated method: nano £100-£350, micro £350-£1,000, mid £1,000-£5,000, macro £5,000-£12,000, mega £12,000-£20,000. On the same page, a USD-converted table (about 0.76 GBP/USD) gives Instagram micro £76-£380, TikTok micro £19-£95, YouTube micro £189-£945, Instagram macro £3,800-£7,600, and YouTube macro £9,450-£18,900. The page does not explain why the two tables disagree. — [Awin UK](https://www.awin.com/gb/sector-insights/uk-influencer-marketing-cost)
- Adobe UK puts micro at about £350-£1,000 per post and mega at £12,000+. — [Adobe UK](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/uk-influencer-marketing-trends)
- Push.fm gives Instagram nano (1-10K) £50-£150, micro (10-50K) £150-£500, mid (50-250K) £500-£2,000, and macro (250K+) £2,000+. — [Push.fm](https://blog.push.fm/?p=20626)
- Kolect (2026, transactional) reports UK and Ireland regional medians per collaboration, converted to USD: TikTok $500, Instagram $662, YouTube $616. The US figures are $450, $450 and $650. — [Kolect influencer rates](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/influencer-rates)

**Germany (EUR)**
- Kolsquare DE (2025) gives Instagram per post: nano €10-€100 (often barter), micro (10-50K) €100-€500, macro €500-€5,000, mega from €10,000. Its other platform figures are in USD only: TikTok micro $25-$125, YouTube micro $200-$1,000, YouTube macro $10,000+. Its FAQ benchmarks are about €10 CPM on Instagram and TikTok "ab 5 € TKP" (from €5 CPM). — [Kolsquare DE 2025](https://www.kolsquare.com/de/blog/wie-viel-kostet-influencer-marketing-im-jahr-2025)
- Shopify DE gives Instagram nano €10-€100, micro (10-50K) €100-€500, and macro (100-500K) €5,000-€10,000, with an average sponsored IG post of about €1,300. For TikTok it gives nano €5-€25, micro €25-€125, and macro (500K-1M) €1,200-€2,500. These TikTok numbers look implausibly low and resemble USD figures relabelled as EUR. — [Shopify DE](https://www.shopify.com/de/blog/influencer-preise)

**France (EUR)**
- Matriochka Influences (the agency's own data: more than 600 paid collaborations with more than 400 influencers, covering 2025 and H1 2026, priced as one feed post plus one story relay, excluding media rights). Median prices for 2025 then H1 2026: micro (10-50K) €1,000 then €3,000 (+200%), mid (50-500K) €3,500 then €4,500, macro (500K-1M) €6,750 then €8,000, mega (1M+) €13,000 then €13,000. The overall median rose from €4,000 to €5,200 (+30%). — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/102945); [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/influence-marketing-tarifs-micro-influenceurs-triple-un-an/)
- BEHYPE (2026) prices a sponsored Instagram Reel at nano €50-€200, micro (10-50K) €2,000-€5,000, and mid (50-500K) €5,000-€15,000. This comes from a search snippet; I did not fetch the page. — [search result set incl. digitiz.fr](https://digitiz.fr/blog/prix-partenariat-influenceur/)
- Another French source gives micro TikTok €200-€1,500 against Instagram €250-€1,750, and puts TikTok 10-40% below Instagram at the same tier. This is also from a search snippet, and I could not pin it to one URL. — [Influee FR TikTok](https://influee.co/fr/blog/tiktok-influencer-rates)

**Australia (AUD)**
- Lilach Bullock gives AUD per feed post: 1-10K $50-$300, 10-50K $200-$800, 50-100K $500-$1,500, 100-500K $1,000-$5,000, 500K-1M $3,000-$15,000, 1M+ $10,000-$50,000+. — [Lilach Bullock AU](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=231562)
- GoodFirms puts micro (10-100K) at AUD $100-$500 per post excluding GST, and macro (500K+) from about AUD $3,000. — [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/blog/micro-vs-macro-influencers-australia)
- Sources disagree on platforms. Influee says TikTok runs below Instagram in AU. Afterpay says TikTok and YouTube creators often command *higher* fees because video costs more to produce. — [Influee AU](https://influee.co/au/blog/influencer-marketing-budget); [Afterpay](https://www.afterpay.com/en-AU/business/access/social-media-tips/influencer-marketing-rates)

**Canada (CAD)**
- I found no credible CAD rate card for Instagram or TikTok. Influee's Canada page (2026, currency unstated) gives Instagram nano $10-$100, micro $100-$500, mid $500-$5,000, macro $5,000+, and TikTok $5-$50, $50-$300, $300-$2,000, $2,000+. — [Influee CA](https://influee.co/ca/blog/influencer-marketing-budget)
- Kolect regional data gives a Canada TikTok median of $600 USD per collaboration, against $450 for the US. — [Kolect influencer rates](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/influencer-rates)

**UAE (AED, brief)**
- Vendor cards (2026) give a wide range. Instagram micro is AED 500-5,000 in one card and AED 2,000-10,000 in Keepface's table. Macro is AED 3,000-20,000 per Reel, or AED 10,000-50,000 per post in other cards. Nano is AED 100-2,000 and mega is AED 15,000-80,000+. — [Keepface UAE 2026](https://keepface.com/site/?p=8751); [UAE Free Zone Finder](https://uaefreezonefinder.com/uae-social-media-influencer-content-creator-guide-2026/)

### Inferences
- The UK figures cluster around micro £200-£1,000 per Instagram post and macro roughly £1,000-£12,000. The low Awin table (£76-£380) is a USD conversion and should be ignored.
- The German EUR cards (micro €100-€500) look like translated USD templates, not German market data. Do not calibrate on them.
- The French Matriochka medians reflect agency-run, premium-brand deals priced as post plus story. They are well above platform or marketplace data (Kolect France median about $560). Use them as a ceiling or "managed campaign" reference, not as the typical price.

### Gaps
- No transaction-based local-currency rate card by tier was found for UK, DE, CA or AU. I found no Billion Dollar Boy, Takumi, Goat, Reachbird or HeyInfluencer public rate cards with numbers.
- No source splits YouTube Shorts from long-form integrations by country.
- No CAD-denominated card from a Canadian agency was found.

## 2. Influencer CPM / cost-per-view by country, and published country multiplier tables

### Takeaway
Kolect (2026, 120K+ agreed collaborations) is the only transactional country table. On a **per-view** basis, the UK, Germany and Australia sit near US parity (0.89-0.97x), France at about 0.78x, Spain and Italy at about 0.63-0.67x, and Canada *above* the US (1.19x). Algorithmic or estimated tables (InfluencerFee) put every market 10-40% below the US. Ad-side YouTube CPMs put the UK, Canada and Germany at only about 0.55-0.65x the US.

### Cited Findings
- **Kolect country table** (partnerships created 19 Aug 2025 to 15 Oct 2026, over 95% in 2026; creator-side fees converted to USD at the ECB rate of 15 Oct 2026; mainly consumer tech, home, DIY and lifestyle brands). Each row gives median fee, then price per 1K average views: US $500 / $27.45; UK $600 / $26.72; Germany $700 / $25.20; France $560 / $21.54; Spain $500 / $18.51; Italy $429 / $17.43; Canada $575 / $32.69; Australia $419 / $24.39. UAE is not included. — [Kolect rates guide](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/influencer-rates); [Kolect CPM benchmarks 2026](https://www.kolect.ai/reports/influencer-cpm-benchmarks-2026)
- Kolect realized CPM (30-day views, median, all countries): TikTok $6.73, Instagram $17.96, YouTube $74.24. By tier, all platforms, price per 1K average views: nano $78.76, micro $30.32, mid $15.68, macro $16.31, mega $10.75. Per-view cost *falls* as creator size rises on TikTok and Instagram. — [Kolect CPM benchmarks 2026](https://www.kolect.ai/reports/influencer-cpm-benchmarks-2026)
- **InfluencerFee** country index (US = 100; Instagram / TikTok / YouTube): UK 80-90 / 75-85 / 80-90; Canada 65-75 / 60-70 / 65-75; Australia 70-80 / 65-75 / 70-80; Germany 65-75 / 55-65 / 65-75; France 60-70 / 55-65 / 60-70; UAE 55-70 / 45-60 / 50-65. The site says the estimates are "generated algorithmically based on publicly available data and industry benchmarks", so these are not transactions. It reviewed the page on 22 Nov 2026. — [InfluencerFee by country](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-pricing-by-country/)
- InfluenceFlow is internally inconsistent. One section puts the UK 10-15% *above* US rates, and another puts Australia 20-30% below. — [InfluenceFlow intl rate cards](https://influenceflow.io/resources/influencer-rate-cards-for-international-campaigns-complete-2025-guide/)
- Infloo's calculator sets each country coefficient as the square root of that country's ad price divided by the US ad price. This gives Germany 0.71x. — [Infloo](https://infloo.io/en/influencer-price-calculator)
- **YouTube ad CPM by country, 2025** (an audience-value proxy). Median dataset: US $11.95, Australia $8.93, UK $7.60, Canada $6.65, Germany $6.43, France $4.54. MilX/AIR creator-platform data: US $14.67, Australia $13.30, Canada $9.93, Germany $9.79, UK $8.91, France $6.76. — [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html); [MilX](https://milx.app/trends/which-countries-have-the-best-youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025)
- Variant gives general paid-media CPMs (2026): US $7-13, UK $6-12, Australia $6-11, Germany $4-8. — [Variant](https://variant-intl.com/blog/average-cpm-by-country.html)

### Inferences
Ratios vs US, computed by me:

| Country | Kolect per-view | Kolect median fee | InfluencerFee (IG midpoint) | YT ad CPM (DIW / MilX) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK | 0.97 | 1.20 | 0.85 | 0.64 / 0.61 |
| Canada | 1.19 | 1.15 | 0.70 | 0.56 / 0.68 |
| Australia | 0.89 | 0.84 | 0.75 | 0.75 / 0.91 |
| Germany | 0.92 | 1.40 | 0.70 | 0.54 / 0.67 |
| France | 0.78 | 1.12 | 0.65 | 0.38 / 0.46 |
| Spain | 0.67 | 1.00 | n/a | n/a |
| Italy | 0.63 | 0.86 | n/a | n/a |
| UAE | n/a | n/a | 0.62 | n/a |

- BVAL prices off views, so **Kolect's per-view ratio is the most relevant input**. Kolect's median-fee ratio is not comparable across countries, because creator size and view mix differ (German creators in the sample are evidently larger).
- Ad-side YouTube CPMs understate creator-deal factors. Creator fees are sticky and set by negotiation, not auction. English-language creators also carry international, largely US, audiences.
- Kolect's sample skews to tech and DIY brands. Canada's 1.19x may reflect a few big US brands buying Canadian creators with US audiences. Treat it as "about parity", not a premium.

### Gaps
- No country-by-tier or country-by-platform CPM table exists in public sources. Kolect gives platform splits only by region.
- I found no published geo-factor tables from HypeAuditor, Modash, Collabstr (country pages), Upfluence or TrySpansa. I did not reach them within the search budget.
- Kolect does not publish per-country sample sizes.

## 3. Audience-geography effect (Tier-1 audience premium)

### Takeaway
Sources agree in direction that buyers price audience geography, not creator location. A US/UK/CA/AU audience is worth more. But no source quantifies a creator-fee multiplier for audience geography. The only numbers are ad CPM ratios.

### Cited Findings
- InfluencerFee advises to "evaluate audience geography, not creator location". It says English-speaking creators in Australia, Canada and the UK offer comparable audience quality at lower rates, and that a Brazilian creator is "poor value for US-market campaigns even at 30% of US rates". — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-pricing-by-country/)
- YouTube ad CPMs show the Tier-1 gap: US about $12-15, AU about $9-13, UK/CA/DE about $6.5-10, FR about $4.5-7, against a global median of about $2.91. — [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html); [MilX](https://milx.app/trends/which-countries-have-the-best-youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025)
- Whito's 2026 UK guide converts USD benchmarks to GBP "adjusted upward for UK audiences". It states no multiplier. — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/?p=10149)

### Inferences
- For code, apply an optional audience-geo adjustment on top of creator-country factors when BVAL knows the audience split. A defensible derived approach is to weight the factor by audience-country share, using the ad-CPM ratios in Section 2 as relative audience values. Shrink these about halfway toward 1.0, since creator deals are less geo-elastic than auctions. Example: UK audience = 1 - 0.5 x (1 - 0.62) ≈ 0.81 per view. This is my construction, not a published figure.
- English-speaking macro and mega creators (UK, CA, AU) often have large US audience shares. This supports moving their factors toward 1.0 at higher tiers.

### Gaps
- I found no published creator-fee multiplier for "% US audience".

## 4. Local add-on norms (usage rights, whitelisting / Partnership / Spark Ads, exclusivity) and agency commission

### Takeaway
I found no market-specific add-on percentages that clearly differ from US norms. European sources quote usage buyouts across very wide ranges: 25-200% of fee in Germany, and an unverified 20-50% in France. French agency medians exclude media rights. Transactional data suggests usage is increasingly bundled into the fee rather than charged on top.

### Cited Findings
- **Germany:** buyouts are typically 25-200% of the content price, depending on duration, type of use (social vs OOH) and geography. Example: a €2,000 post plus one month of brand-account use costs a €500 buyout (25%). Exclusivity raises price, but no figure is given. Affiliate deals pay 10-15% of sales, tiered by redemptions. — [Kolsquare DE 2025](https://www.kolsquare.com/de/blog/wie-viel-kostet-influencer-marketing-im-jahr-2025)
- **France:** Matriochka's reference medians are "hors droits de médiatisation", so paid-media usage is extra. — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/102945). One French search snippet says usage rights typically add 20-50%, but I could not attribute it to a specific page, so treat it as unverified.
- **UK:** Awin lists whitelisting, cross-channel use, multi-market activation and longer licensing windows as costs beyond the organic fee, and warns that "whitelisting without pre-agreed rights inflates costs later". It gives no percentages. It cites agency campaign costs of about £500-£20,000. — [Awin UK](https://www.awin.com/gb/sector-insights/uk-influencer-marketing-cost)
- **UK talent and agency commission:** a talent manager's cut is usually about 10-20%, which Kolsquare says should already be included in the creator's quote. Kolsquare also says UK benchmarks for agency pricing are hard to find and the only dedicated benchmarks are from the US. — [Kolsquare UK agencies pricing](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/blog/how-influencer-agencies-price-retainers-commission-creator-fees)
- **Cross-market (mostly US) data:** in Kolect's data, 80.4% of single-post deals included paid usage, whitelisting or ad-code rights, with the same $500 median as organic-only deals, so no visible premium. Aspire: 67% of brands bundle usage into the initial rate. Collabstr (2024): $307 with usage vs $221 without (+39%). IMH: whitelisting costs 20-30% of base fee per month, and category exclusivity adds 15-30%. — [Kolect](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/influencer-rates)
- Digiday reports usage priced as a flat monthly fee or as a percentage of paid media spend. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/marketers-say-usage-rights-are-driving-up-the-price-to-work-with-creators/)

### Inferences
- Keep BVAL's US add-on percentages globally, and apply them to the market-adjusted base. The only local tweak with any support is that German buyouts may be quoted as larger multiples for long, OOH or multi-country terms. Cap at 200% for perpetual or OOH use in DE.
- Spain, Italy and France rate cards usually quote ex-rights. Make sure FR/ES/IT outputs label usage as excluded by default.

### Gaps
- No country-specific Spark Ads or Partnership Ads fee norms were found.
- No country-specific exclusivity premiums were found.
- No agency commission benchmarks were found for DE, FR, CA or AU.

## 5. Derived market factors per tier and country (for code)

### Takeaway
**DERIVED.** This is a blended per-view factor to multiply BVAL's US CPMs. The blend is 50% Kolect per-view ratio (transactional), 25% InfluencerFee index (estimate), and 25% the mean of the two YouTube ad-CPM ratios (audience value). Tier offsets are my judgment, set lower at nano and micro and closer to 1.0 at macro and mega for English-language markets, where creators carry international audiences. Each figure is a midpoint, not a cited number.

### Cited Findings
- The inputs are the cited figures in Section 2: [Kolect](https://www.kolect.ai/reports/influencer-cpm-benchmarks-2026), [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-pricing-by-country/), [DIW](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html) and [MilX](https://milx.app/trends/which-countries-have-the-best-youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025).
- TikTok vs Instagram: InfluencerFee puts TikTok about 5-10 index points below Instagram in every market. — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-pricing-by-country/). French sources put TikTok 10-40% below Instagram. — [Influee FR](https://influee.co/fr/blog/tiktok-influencer-rates). Kolect's UK/IE regional medians run the other way, with TikTok ($500) relatively *closer* to US ($450) than Instagram is ($662 vs $450). — [Kolect](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/influencer-rates)

### Inferences

Blended base factors (derived): UK 0.85, CA 0.92 → use 0.90, AU 0.84, DE 0.79, FR 0.66. Spain and Italy use Kolect only with a 0.9 haircut, giving about 0.60 and 0.57. UAE uses InfluencerFee only, about 0.60.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom (GBP) | 0.80 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.95 | Medium |
| Canada (CAD) | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.95 | 0.95 | Medium-low (sources conflict: Kolect 1.19 vs InfluencerFee 0.70) |
| Australia (AUD) | 0.80 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.90 | Medium |
| Germany (EUR) | 0.75 | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.85 | Medium-low |

| UAE (AED) | 0.55 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.65 | 0.70 | Low (estimates only) |

Optional platform modifiers on top (derived):
- **TikTok:** x0.95 in UK, CA and AU, and x0.90 in DE, FR, ES, IT and UAE, based on InfluencerFee's 5-10 point gap. The Kolect UK data contradicts this, so a neutral 1.0 is also defensible.
- **YouTube integration:** x1.0. InfluencerFee puts YouTube on par with Instagram, and Kolect UK YouTube is about 0.95x US.
- **Shorts:** no country data. Use 1.0.

Implementation notes:
- Apply the factor to USD output, then convert with a live FX feed. Kolect used ECB rates; Awin implies about 0.76 GBP/USD in Jan 2026. Do not hardcode FX.
- Sanity check, UK micro: a 50K-follower creator with about 15K Reel views gives US $20 x 15 = $300. With the 0.85 factor that is about $255, or roughly £195. This sits at the low end of the £200-£1,000 UK micro Instagram cards from Kolsquare and Adobe. If BVAL output should match "quoted" rate cards rather than negotiated medians, the UK factors could move up 0.05-0.10.
- Expose the factor as configurable and show it in output. Tag it "derived (Kolect 2026 + InfluencerFee 2026 + YouTube ad-CPM 2025)".

### Gaps
- The tier offsets are not supported by country-by-tier data. They are judgment calls.
- Spain, Italy and UAE factors rest on one source each.
- Canada's direction is genuinely uncertain: one source puts it above the US and another 30% below. Consider defaulting it to 0.90 and revisiting if Collabstr or HypeAuditor Canada data becomes available.
