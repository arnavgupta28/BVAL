# BVAL country selection and per-country practicalities (as of 22 Nov 2026)

## Q1. How do candidate countries rank for a creator-side pricing tool (spend, creators, platform users)?

### Takeaway
On money spent with creators, the US is far ahead of everyone else (43% of global creator spend). The UK (5%) and Germany (4%) come next, then Brazil, France and Canada (2% each), then India and Australia (1% each). On people reached, the order is very different: India, the US, Indonesia and Brazil have by far the biggest combined Instagram + TikTok + YouTube ad audiences, followed by Mexico, the Philippines, Japan and Turkey. The countries that appear high on both lists are the US, India, Brazil and the UK.

## Q2. Which 5–8 countries should BVAL support first, and why?

### Takeaway
**Core 5, in rank order: US, India, UK, Brazil, Germany.** Together they hold about 55% of global creator spend, and they include three of the four largest platform audiences. India and the US are already built.

**Extended to 8: Indonesia, Mexico, Canada.** Indonesia and Mexico have very large TikTok and YouTube user bases even though brand budgets there are small. Canada is about 2% of spend and costs almost nothing to add (English, USD-adjacent).

**Alternates:** France (2% of spend, but French language and strict labelling law), Australia (1% of spend, highest YouTube CPM, English), and Japan (large YouTube and Instagram audiences, but no spend data and Japanese-only business culture).

### Cited Findings
- Spend shares: US 43, UK 5, DE 4, BR 2, FR 2, CA 2, IN 1, AU 1 (%, 2025). India is forecast to grow fastest in 2026, at +20% — [Net Influencer / Madison and Wall, 19 Nov 2026](https://www.netinfluencer.com/advertiser-spending-on-creators-to-reach-116b-usd-in-2026-report-shows/)
- Audience leaders: India, the US, Brazil and Indonesia appear in the top 4 for both YouTube and Instagram. Indonesia, Brazil, the US and Mexico are the top 4 for TikTok — [DataReportal IG](https://datareportal.com/essential-instagram-stats), [TikTok](https://datareportal.com/essential-tiktok-stats), [YouTube](https://datareportal.com/essential-youtube-stats)
- Relative rate levels (US = 100): UK about 85, DE about 65, CA about 70, AU about 75, FR about 62, BR about 37, MX about 32, IN about 22, Indonesia/SEA about 15 — [InfluencerFee 2026](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-pricing-by-country/)

### Inferences
- Scoring method: equal weight on (a) share of global creator spend and (b) combined platform reach.
  - US, India, Brazil and the UK clear the bar on both.
  - Germany wins on spend (4%, the third-largest named market) and its YouTube reach (68.8M).
  - Indonesia and Mexico win only on reach.
  - Canada wins only on spend, but it costs almost nothing to add.
- Tradeoff for the product:
  - High-volume, low-rate markets (India, Indonesia, Mexico, Brazil) will bring the most users. They need their own local rate bases, because US rates scaled down by FX would be wildly too high.
  - High-spend markets (UK, DE, CA) need only a factor on the US base plus local VAT and disclosure wording.
- Canada and Australia could be handled by the "Tier-1 English" fallback group (see Q6) instead of named modes. Doing that frees two named slots for France and Japan if wanted.

### Gaps
- Japan's creator spend share is not reported by Madison and Wall, so its rank is uncertain.
- No source gives the share of creators who use pricing tools, or the share of talent managers, by country.

- **Indonesia:** PPN (VAT) **12%**, per PwC (last reviewed 11 Jun 2026) — [PwC Tax Summaries VAT quick chart](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/quick-charts/value-added-tax-vat-rates)

### Inferences
- **Display rounding (my proposal, not sourced):**
  - USD / GBP / EUR / CAD / AUD: whole units; round fees to 25 or 50 below 1k and to 50 or 100 above 1k.
  - INR: Indian grouping (₹1,50,000), rounded to ₹500 below ₹50k and ₹1,000 above.
  - BRL / MXN: round to 50 or 100.
  - IDR: no decimals, rounded to Rp100,000 (Rp1 juta is about $55); show "Rp 2,5 juta" style if possible.
  - JPY: whole yen, rounded to ¥1,000.
- **Ex-tax quoting:** B2B creator quotes in VAT/GST countries are conventionally ex-tax ("+ GST" / "zzgl. MwSt." / "+ VAT"). BVAL should label output "ex-tax" and add a tax line only when the creator says they are registered. US services generally carry no sales tax. That is my inference; I did not verify it per state.
- Because the Fed figure is an October average, **BVAL should fetch live FX at run time** (e.g. ECB or open.er-api) and keep the table above only as a fallback.

### Gaps
- **Not verified in sources retrieved here (from background knowledge; confirm before shipping):**
  - France VAT 20%
  - Mexico IVA 16%
  - UK VAT registration threshold £90,000
  - Indonesia's effective 11% PPN on non-luxury services (12% headline rate applied to an 11/12 tax base since 2025)
  - Brazil ISS of 2–5% (municipal) on creator services until the reform completes
- No mid-November 2026 dated daily FX was retrieved for IDR from a central bank.

## Q5. Disclosure / ad law per country (one line each, for email and red flags)

### Takeaway
Every recommended market requires clear ad labelling on sponsored posts.
- **UK:** the ASA/CAP/CMA guide was updated in Oct 2026, and the recommended labels are still "#ad"-style.
- **Brazil:** CONAR's new influencer guide took effect 1 Jun 2026 and treats affiliate commissions as advertising.
- **India:** ASCI prescribes a fixed list of labels.

### Cited Findings
- **US (FTC):** requires a prominent, clearly visible disclosure when there is a material connection. Low-value gifts may not need disclosure in the US, a case that is more complex under German law — [NYU JIPEL, US vs Germany disclosure](https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/disclosure-requirements-for-influencer-marketing-in-the-u-s-and-germany/)
- **UK (ASA/CAP + CMA):** ads must be "obviously identifiable". Use #Ad / #Advert / #Advertising / #Advertisement / #AdvertisementFeature; avoid "Supported by", "Funded by" and "Gifted" alone. A new edition of the joint guide was published Oct 2026. The CMA requires disclosure for any payment, loan or incentive — [ASA influencers' guide](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html); [Lewis Silkin, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/23/new-influencer-guidance-from-the-asa-cap-and-cma-what-has-changed-and-what-to-d-102o6ar); [ASA #Ad(vice)](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ad-vice-making-clear-that-an-ad-is-an-ad.html)
- **India (ASCI):** a label is mandatory, chosen only from: Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, or Instagram's "Paid Partnership". It must not be buried in hashtags. Videos of 15 seconds or less with no caption need an on-screen label for at least 3 seconds — [ASCI Influencer Guidelines PDF](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf); [ASCI dos and don'ts](https://www.ascionline.in/social/dos-dont/)
- **Brazil (CONAR):** the new *Guia de Marketing e Publicidade por Influenciadores Digitais* was published 13 May 2026 and is effective 1 Jun 2026. Affiliate or performance pay counts as advertising, AI-made content does not remove responsibility, and brands share liability. The guide is self-regulation, not statute — [Propmark](https://propmark.com.br/artigos/o-novo-guia-do-conar-para-influenciadores-e-os-riscos-da-falta-de-transparencia/); [Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/artigos/77061/conar-novas-regras-para-influenciadores-e-ia-a-partir-de-1o-de-junho/); [UAI, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.uai.com.br/networking-e-negocios/2026/09/23/ganha-comissao-com-link-de-afiliado-para-o-conar-isso-ja-e-publicidade/)
- **Germany:** requires a prominent, visible ad label (conventionally "Werbung"/"Anzeige"); stricter than the US on gifted products — [NYU JIPEL](https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/disclosure-requirements-for-influencer-marketing-in-the-u-s-and-germany/)
- **Canada (Ad Standards):** use clear tags such as #ad / #sponsored, not #partner or #brand. Put the disclosure up front; a one-time bio disclosure is not enough — [CreatorDB, English-speaking regulation](https://creatordb.app/blog/regulation-english/)
- **Australia:** a duty to disclose exists but rules on how to disclose are not codified; "Ad"/"Advertisement" is recommended — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/blog/regulation-english/)
- **EU:** consumer-protection law applies across the EU. France mandates specific disclosure wording. Spain has an influencer-specific law for creators earning more than €300k a year or with more than 1M followers. Brand and creator can both be liable — [DLA Piper global guide, Sep 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/news/2025/09/dla-piper-unpacks-influencer-marketing-rules-with-updated-and-expanded-global-guide); [Lewis Silkin / search summary](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2025/11/the-global-influencer-crackdown-a-2025-guide-to-new-advertising-regulations/)

### Inferences
- **Generic red flag for every market:** the brand asks for no ad label, or for "#partner"/"gifted"-only wording.
- **Market-specific flags:**
  - Brazil: an affiliate-only deal with no label.
  - India: a label outside the ASCI list.
  - UK: "Supported by" alone.
- **Email line template:** "Content will carry the [market label] disclosure required by [regulator]."

### Gaps
- Mexico and Indonesia disclosure rules were not researched within the tool budget. Mexico's PROFECO/Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor and Indonesia's ad ethics code (EPI) are likely relevant; unverified.
- The current text of the FTC Endorsement Guides was not fetched directly.

### Gaps
- No primary or official per-country influencer-rate dataset exists in public sources. Both proxies are commercial and their methods are opaque (InfluencerFee is algorithmic; RevenueLab is creator-reported).
- Russia was not covered by the CPM sources.
- The Gulf factor is the least certain: the two CPM sources differ by about 3x for Saudi Arabia.
